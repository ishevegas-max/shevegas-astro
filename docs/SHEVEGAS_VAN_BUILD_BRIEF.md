## The One-Liner

SheVegas is a living, open-world story engine — Zelda meets Willy Wonka meets Alice in Wonderland meets every deep-cut piece of American pop culture from the last hundred years — seeded in Sheboygan (53081) and built to replicate anywhere.

---

## The Core Idea

Not journalism. Not a game. A **world people walk into and add to**.

- You stumble in from 53081.
- The world generates around you — a room that *is* Lidsville, a character who talks like a '70s sitcom dad because that's literally who he is.
- You gather clues, facts, knowledge. The story outlives the renderer.
- **Anyone can add.** Facts and fragments go through a verification loop (not just notification — actual trust mechanics). Verified stuff becomes part of the world. The world literally changes based on what the community validates.

The buy-in is the moat. Once people have invested their own facts and stories, they don't just play it — they defend it. That's a tribe, not a user base. Tribes scale.

---

## The Tone Stack

- **Harry Nilsson — The Point!** as the emotional spine. Narrator who walks you through it, half-singing, half-explaining. Me and My Arrow energy.
- **Willy Wonka** — the eccentric host who built the place and dares you to keep up. Rooms that shouldn't exist.
- **Alice in Wonderland** — you don't choose the adventure, it chooses you. Every door leads somewhere weirder.
- **Snow White & the Seven Dwarves, Adam West Batman, Lidsville, That '70s Show, Partridge Family, Marty McFly** — the deep cuts, not the canon hits. The obscure stuff is what makes it feel alive instead of like a museum.
- **Urkel, MC Hammer, anything** — soft-shoot every piece of pop culture. A guy in hammer pants doing the running man is just Tuesday here.

The in-betweens are where the real story lives. Oompa Loompas who know the factory better than the boss. The Cheshire Cat who exists outside the rules. NPCs who are neither quest-givers nor villains — just people with their own reasons for being there.

## The Van (Mobile Outlet Store)

Sheboygan is the off-ramp into the Hitchhiker's Guide. The Van is the portable version of the outlet store.

**Layers:**
1. **Shell** — a web app that runs on any phone or tablet. No install, no friction. That's the storefront.
2. **Engine** — the clue economy, the verification loop, the world generator that spins up from 53081.
3. **Skin** — the pop culture layers, the soundtrack, the in-betweens.

Instead of guilt cards (like Dreambeans / Cool Beans — "you should be doing this amazing thing today"), the Van hands out **cool beans**: little verified clues, pop-culture fragments, or community facts that actually fit your view from 53081. You grab one, it opens a door into the world.

---

## The Clue Economy (Core Loop)

- Player finds or submits a clue/fact.
- It goes through verification — community trust mechanics, not just a thumbs up.
- Verified = it becomes part of the world. The world changes.
- Players earn trust by confirming or challenging claims.
- The obscure pop-culture stuff is the texture. The real stuff is the content engine.

---

## Scaling

Sheboygan is the seed, not the ceiling. Prove the loop in one town. Then hand the same engine to any place that wants it. Every city gets its own world, its own tribe, its own story. The platform doesn't scale by adding features — it scales by **replication**. One world becomes a hundred, a thousand, and each one feeds the others.

That's not a product. That's a movement with a map.
